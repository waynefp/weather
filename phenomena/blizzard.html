Formation Science

Cold Air, Moisture & the Storm Track

A blizzard requires two ingredients that are rarely found together: extremely cold arctic air and abundant moisture. The collision of these air masses along a powerful low-pressure system creates the explosive snowfall and violent winds that define the phenomenon.

Alberta Clippers are fast-moving systems that sweep southeast out of Canada, bringing intense cold and moderate snow. They typically lack deep moisture — their fury comes from speed and wind. Nor'easters, in contrast, draw moisture from the Atlantic Ocean along the US East Coast, producing prodigious snowfall sometimes exceeding 60 cm, alongside fierce northeasterly winds.

Lake Effect Snow is a uniquely intense regional phenomenon. As frigid Arctic air masses cross the relatively warm Great Lakes, they absorb enormous moisture and heat from the open water. When this air reaches the downwind shore, it drops its moisture as intense, narrow bands of heavy snow — sometimes at rates exceeding 10 cm per hour. Cities like Buffalo, New York, and Syracuse routinely receive annual snowfalls of 3–4 metres from this effect alone.

The wind chill calculation captures how cold the air feels on exposed skin, combining actual temperature with wind speed. At –20°C with 50 km/h winds, the wind chill index reaches approximately –37°C — frostbite on exposed skin occurs within 10 minutes.

Science & Structure

Crystals, Chill & Lake Effect

Snow Crystal Types

Temperature and humidity determine crystal shape: –2°C produces thin plates; –5°C makes needles and columns; –15°C creates the classic six-armed dendrites; –30°C produces hollow columns. No two complex dendrites are identical due to slightly different atmospheric paths.

Lake Effect Snow

Buffalo, NY averages 2.5 m of snow per year, much of it lake-effect. The record single event was 175 cm from a 1977 storm. Narrow bands can dump intense snow for hours on one neighbourhood while the next is clear.

Wind Chill Index

The modern formula (adopted 2001) uses face-level wind at 1.5 m height. At –30°C and 60 km/h, wind chill reaches –51°C. Frostbite to exposed skin in under 5 minutes.

Notable Events

Historic Winter Storms

Buffalo Blizzard — 2022

A bomb cyclone struck Buffalo, NY over Christmas 2022. Winds exceeding 100 km/h combined with heavy lake-effect snow produced white-out conditions for 48 hours. 37 people died, many trapped in cars. Snowfall totals exceeded 150 cm in some areas within 72 hours.

Storm of the Century — 1993

Struck the entire US East Coast in March 1993. From Cuba to Canada, 300 fatalities, 10 million without power. Every major East Coast airport closed simultaneously. The storm produced tornadoes in Florida and blizzard conditions from Alabama to Maine — an unprecedented meteorological event.

Snowmageddon — 2010

Two back-to-back nor'easters paralysed Washington D.C. with 90 cm of snow in five days. The federal government shut down for a week. "Snowmageddon" entered popular culture. Despite the disruption, it highlighted how climate shifts can bring more intense snowfall events even as overall winters warm.
